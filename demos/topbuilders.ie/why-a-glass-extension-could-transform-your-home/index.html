Extension design

Planning a glass-led extension without compromising comfort

Glass can connect a home to the garden and change the quality of daylight. It also has to work as part of the structure and thermal envelope.

Updated 16 July 20266 min read

Begin with orientation

The same glazed elevation behaves differently when it faces north, south, east or west. Direct summer sun, low evening sun, neighbour positions and existing trees all affect glare, overheating, privacy and the usefulness of the space through the day.

Daylight should reach the original house

A bright new room can leave the old middle room comparatively dark. Rooflights, ceiling height, internal openings, surface reflectance and the location of solid walls should be used to distribute light through the combined plan.

Structure determines how light the junction can feel

Large openings and slim visual lines still need support. Steel position, deflection, door systems, roof edges and drainage should be coordinated before the architectural detail is promised.

Comfort is a design requirement

Glass specification, shading, opening vents, background ventilation and heating all contribute. A large glazed roof is not automatically better than carefully positioned rooflights and insulated roof area.

Ask the useful question: not “How much glass can we add?” but “Where will glass improve daylight, outlook and movement without making the room difficult to heat, cool or furnish?”

Planning and neighbour impact

Highly glazed extensions can affect overlooking and perceived privacy even when their physical size is modest. Review the design against current local policy and the individual site. South Dublin County Council’s house extension design guide is a useful example of the design issues local authorities expect homeowners to consider.

Details to settle before ordering glazing

  • Final structural opening and tolerances
  • Finished floor levels and drainage
  • Threshold and weathering strategy
  • Glass performance specification
  • Solar-control and shading approach
  • Opening panels and ventilation
  • Access for delivery and lifting
  • Interfaces with kitchen, floor and ceiling finishes

The best glass-led extensions feel calm because the technical decisions are absorbed into the architecture.
