Attic conversions

A practical guide to attic conversions in Ireland

A roof space is not automatically a room. Feasibility starts with geometry, structure, access and the safety route through the existing house.

Updated 16 July 20268 min read

Measure the roof before planning the room

Usable headroom is shaped by roof pitch, ridge height, purlins, chimneys and the depth of the new insulated build-up. A plan can look generous while the section reveals that circulation and furniture would be compressed into a narrow strip.

The first review should record the roof structure, clear heights, likely stair zone and the rooms directly below.

The stair is part of the whole-house plan

An attic stair needs safe geometry and a sensible arrival point. It also takes space from the floor below. A conversion that creates one room but damages a bedroom or circulation route downstairs may not be a good trade.

Structure and fire safety are not finishing details

Existing ceiling joists are not normally treated as a new habitable floor without structural assessment. Roof alterations, dormers and stair openings also affect load paths and temporary support.

The Department of Housing’s current Loft Conversion Guidelines highlight principal issues including fire safety. Use those guidelines as a starting point and appoint the appropriate professionals for the specific house.

Important: do not advertise or use an attic as a compliant bedroom solely because it has been finished attractively. Intended use and compliance need project-specific confirmation.

Planning and building requirements are separate questions

Some roof works may require planning permission, while building regulations apply to the construction work whether or not planning permission is required. Dormer position, roof alterations, overlooking and the property’s planning context should be checked before design assumptions become fixed.

Comfort needs a complete envelope

Insulation, airtightness, ventilation, roof-space moisture and summer temperature interact. Adding insulation without resolving ventilation and junctions can create different problems. If grant-funded insulation is being considered, check current eligibility and process directly with SEAI before starting work; grant approval may be required in advance.

A useful attic-conversion brief

  • Intended room use
  • Existing roof survey and section
  • Proposed stair position
  • Structural strategy
  • Fire and escape strategy
  • Rooflight or dormer proposal
  • Insulation, ventilation and airtightness details
  • Storage and eaves access
  • Plumbing and drainage route if a bathroom is proposed

The right outcome may be a rooflight room, a dormer, a more substantial roof intervention—or a decision that another type of extension serves the home better.
